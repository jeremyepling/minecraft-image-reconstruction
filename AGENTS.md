When an experiment changes reconstruction behavior or reveals a failure worth testing in training, add a dated bullet to [the reconstruction adjustment log](MODEL_CHANGELOG.md). Record the decision, measured effect and example, and link the replay or evidence. Distinguish model changes from rules and evaluation changes.

Follow the [product intent](PRODUCT_INTENT.md): reconstruct only blocks with visible face evidence in the supplied image or images. Jeremy does not want hidden-area prediction in either this pipeline or the proposed training approach. Do not complete unseen backs, interiors, occluded surfaces, shells or concealed supports. Placing the full Minecraft cube for a visibly supported block is allowed; adding unsupported cells is not. Leave unobserved areas unknown and omitted, rather than labeling them air or clearing them in an existing world.

For a single-image run, visibility is defined by that input alone. Other captured angles and full source-world data may generate labels and score results, but must not leak hidden geometry into prediction or supervision targets. Train and evaluate visible geometry, grids, materials and placements with explicit visibility masks; exclude unobserved cells from occupancy and material losses rather than treating them as empty. Do not introduce completion, inpainting or full-world reconstruction objectives. The legacy `--depth` shell option remains historical behavior outside this intent; documentation changes do not imply that runtime enforcement has been implemented.

Use stage and method names in conversation and new reviews. The **Surface Finder** groups visible pixels; **Regions First** is its mask-based method. The legacy four-corner method is the **Rectangle Finder** (labeled B in archived results). The **Wall Separator** refines masks using image edges, relative depth and optional rectangle hints. The **Underside Finder** can create a thin surface from line and shading evidence. Keep **Surface Straightener**, **Block Counter** and **Material Matcher** distinct. Use the source manifest to identify which method actually ran.
